# jLock privacy policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026

jLock is an app lock that works fully offline.

**What jLock stores.** Your lock methods, the list of apps you lock, the apps whose notifications you hide, your settings (theme and lock options), and an activity log of failed unlocks and hidden notifications (the app's name and the time). PINs, patterns and passwords are never stored as entered: jLock keeps only a salted one-way hash, encrypted with a key held in your phone's secure hardware. All of it stays in jLock's private storage on your phone.

**What jLock sends.** Nothing. jLock has no internet access, no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. "Check for updates" only opens the release page in your own browser; jLock itself sends nothing.

**Which app is in front.** To show the lock, jLock checks which app is in front, using either Usage Access or Accessibility (you choose). It uses this only at that moment to decide whether to show the lock. It does not read, record or send what is on your screen.

**Notifications.** If you turn on hidden notifications, jLock looks only at which app a new notification came from and what kind it is (for example, an ongoing call is left alone), so it can replace it with "New notification". It does not read the notification's title or text.

**Fingerprint.** Fingerprint unlock uses Android's own prompt. jLock only learns whether the check passed; it never sees your fingerprint.

**Backups and new phones.** jLock's data is excluded from Android cloud backup and phone-to-phone transfer, so your lock never leaves the phone.

**Deleting your data.** Clear the activity log from the log screen. Uninstalling jLock, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes all of its data.

**Children.** jLock is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

**Changes.** If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this address with a new date.
